How the plate is drawn

The edge, by Fourier

Any closed curve is a sum of circles turning at whole-number speeds. The brass machine is that sum made literal: one circle for each coefficient ck, largest first, the pen at the end of the chain.

γ(x) = Σck eikx,   k ≡ σ (mod m)

Keeping every frequency in one residue class mod m gives the curve m-fold symmetry, whatever the amplitudes and phases. Those phases drift slowly, so the plate never quite repeats.

The depth, by Laplace

Treat the curve as the edge of a half-cylinder and ask for the steady state that edge would impose, the way heat or electric potential settles. That is Laplace's equation, and its bounded solution damps each harmonic at a rate set by its frequency:

uxx + uyy = 0  ⇒  u(x, y) = Σck e−|k|y eikx

Fine detail dies first. Deep enough, only the slowest circle is left, and every point sinks toward c0, the mean of the edge. With r = e−y this is the Poisson integral of the curve on the unit disc.
